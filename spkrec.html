For my diploma thesis at the Circuits & Systems Lab of NTUA, I designed and built an autonomous, text-independent speaker recognition system running entirely on an STM32F429 microcontroller (ARM Cortex-M4, 180 MHz).

The system extracts Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) from short frames of speech and classifies them with a feed-forward neural network. A decision metric then picks the speaker based on how many frames were matched to each person. It reached over 90% accuracy across the lab's ~10 members.

I also built the hardware and firmware: an I²S digital microphone, an SD card over 4-bit SDIO with FatFs and DMA, and external ROM. Early prototypes used an analog microphone sampled through the ADC with DMA, plus an SPI display for testing.

Tools

  • C, Python
  • STM32CubeMX, Atollic TrueSTUDIO
  • Altium Designer
  • Logic analyzer
